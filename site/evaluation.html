How to read this page

The evaluation runs every week in GitHub Actions over the last 30 days of collected data. Every number shows its sample size (n) and the period it covers. Numbers based on fewer than 30 observations are marked preliminary: they can change a lot as data accumulates. Negative or inconclusive results are reported as they are.

Limitations

  • MTA service alerts are an imperfect ground truth for disruptions: some delays get no alert, and alerts can be late or stay up after service recovers.
  • The surface data covers what free public feeds cover: traffic sensors mostly on highways and arterials, cameras only during events, and bus speeds only with a working Bus Time key.
  • Baselines need about three weeks of history, so early results rely on few comparable days.
  • Train arrivals are observed at poll times about a minute apart, with longer gaps when GitHub delays scheduled runs, so realized waits are approximate.
  • Differences near disruptions are associations; the placebo test helps rule out chance and city-wide effects but does not prove causation.
