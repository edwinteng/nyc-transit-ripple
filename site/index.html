    About

    When the New York City subway is disrupted, riders spill onto the streets at the same time, switching to buses, ride-hailing or Citi Bike, and the most obvious alternative can become congested in turn. This dashboard tracks that ripple using only free public data: MTA subway GTFS-Realtime feeds and service alerts, the MTA static schedule, NYC DOT real-time traffic speeds, NYC DOT traffic camera images, MTA Bus Time bus positions, and Citi Bike station status. Every few minutes a GitHub Actions pipeline computes how late trains are against the schedule; a line has a delay event when its median train delay stays at or above 5 minutes for 5 consecutive minutes. For the most affected stations it compares traffic speed, bus speed, camera counts of vehicles and people (an open-source YOLOX detector that keeps numbers only, never images) and Citi Bike availability within 500 m with the usual level for the same weekday and time of day, and combines them into a 0-100 surface impact score. While a disruption is ongoing it ranks the ways to leave each affected station (another line, Citi Bike, bus, or waiting, based on how long similar past disruptions lasted) and flags the obvious option when it already looks crowded. Any past event can be replayed minute by minute, and the Evaluation page reports how well all of this works, with sample sizes. The explanations are written by fixed templates and only quote numbers present in the data. Limitations: scheduled runs can be delayed, some trips cannot be matched to the schedule, traffic sensors cover mainly highways and arterials and are sometimes hours behind, cameras undercount small or dark scenes, and baselines need several weeks of history before comparisons are reliable. Differences from usual conditions describe what happened near a disruption; they do not prove that the disruption caused them.
